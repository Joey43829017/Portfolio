🧮 Physics Foundations

Moment of Inertia (Eq. 1) I = Σ(mᵢ × rᵢ²)

Higher MOI reduces rotational penalty on off-center hits

Dynamic Loft (Eq. 2) θ_dynamic = θ_static + Δθ_deflection

Minimizing dynamic loft variation reduces three-putt frequency

Gravitational Torque (Eq. 3) τ_gravity = m × g × r_CG × sin(φ)

Optimizing initial backspin shortens skid-roll transition

🔬 Materials & Manufacturing

Club Head

Material: 6061 Aluminum (Industry Standard)

Rationale: Balance of cost efficiency and machinability. Provides exceptional feel while striking the ball.

Tolerance: ±0.005 in precision machining

Weighting System

Material: 304 Stainless Steel

Rationale: Corrosion resistance for outdoor exposure. Originally 303 considered, but 304 provides better weather protection.

Implementation: Distributed to club perimeter to maximize MOI

Surface Finish

Roughness: 125 µin (2.3 µm) Ra minimum

Treatment: Black Anodize coating

Edge Treatment: All sharp edges broken, burrs removed

⚡ What is Zero Torque Design?

The Zero Torque design is balanced in all directions around the shaft. When the putter is balanced on the shaft regardless of orientation, it will not self-correct because one side of the club always has the same weight as the opposite side. This design ensures that:

  • The shaft intersects with the center of gravity — critical for eliminating unwanted rotation
  • Center-shafted placement provides maximum comfort and accuracy (validated at Golf Galaxy)
  • Symmetrical weight distribution eliminates torque-induced face rotation during stroke

PROJECT HIGHLIGHT

The Lazy Man Alarm Snoozer

ME 2900 Arduino Prototype Project · The Ohio State University

The Problem

Waking up on a Monday morning to the sound of a blaring alarm is unpleasant — and turning it off requires physical movement out of bed, which is difficult for people with mobility issues or simply those who don't want to move. The goal was simple: turn off an alarm without leaving your bed.

The Solution

An infrared remote and servo motor drive a robotic arm that presses the power button on your phone, silencing the alarm from across the room. A sound sensor confirms an actual alarm is ringing before actuating — preventing false triggers from ambient noise.

📡

Infrared Remote (NEC)

Wireless wake trigger; reads NEC protocol command codes. Only 1 digital pin required on the Arduino.

🤖

Servo Motor (Digital Pin 9)

Rotates arm 135° to reliably contact and press the phone's power button.

🔊

Sound Sensor Module

Analog threshold detection (>75% analog value) confirms a real alarm is ringing.

💡

Yellow LED (Pin 3)

Visual confirmation that the sound sensor is active and monitoring.

How It Works

  1. I wake the device: Press any button on the IR remote — the receiver (NEC protocol, pin 2) signals the Arduino to enable the system.
  2. The alarm sounds: The sound sensor picks up audio frequencies near the phone's speaker. Analog values are mapped to a 0–100% percentage scale; above ~75% indicates an active alarm.
  3. The arm actuates: When the threshold is exceeded, the servo motor sweeps from 0° → 135° at 25ms intervals (smooth motion), then returns — pressing the phone's power button to silence it.
  4. Status feedback: The yellow LED confirms sound-sensor activation, and the LCD displays messages like "Good Night, Sleep Tight!" via a shift register (reducing MCU pin usage).

Engineering Notes & Challenges

Servo jitter fix: The servo initially jitted due to redundant setup commands in the sketch (manually setting pinMode OUTPUT + digitalWrite). The Servo.h library handles initialization automatically — removing those lines eliminated the issue.

Baud rate: Serial communication runs at 115200 baud to accommodate the IR remote's NEC decoder.

Servicing threshold calibration: The sound sensor's optimal threshold was user-calibrated against multiple alarm sounds; ~7% of full capacity reliably triggers actuation without false positives from room tone.
