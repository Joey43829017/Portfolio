Zero Torque Putter Design

Physics-Based Golf Putter Engineering

Problem Statement

The goal was to design a precision golf putter head with zero torque characteristics through the face, providing consistent distance control and improving putt accuracy. Traditional putters often produce unwanted face rotation (torque) during impact, leading to inconsistent ball direction.

Key Challenges:

  • Achieving MOI ≥5000 g·cm² for stability
  • Implementing adjustable toe hang (20°)
  • Maintaining optimal COR across face impact zone
  • Low CG position for immediate forward roll

Engineering Approach

The design leveraged physics-based analysis using Fusion 360 CAD, followed by 3D printing for rapid prototyping. The approach included:

Moment of Inertia Requirements:

Ixx ≥ 5500 g·cm² (horizontal axis)
Ihosel ≥ 3000 g·cm² (hosel point)

Center of Gravity Specifications:

CG Height ≤ 18mm (for low profile)
CG Depth: -12 to -18mm (rear bias)

Torque Analysis:

τ = r × F
where minimizing τ through optimized face geometry and weight distribution eliminates unwanted rotation.

Design Features:

  • Milled or grooved face for consistent COR (0.85 ± 0.02)
  • Toe hang adjustable from 5°-35° via weight ports
  • Optimized head geometry for minimal rotational inertia

Calculations and Analysis

Moment of Inertia was calculated using the parallel axis theorem:

I = Icm + md²

Where:
Icm = Mass moment of inertia about center of mass
m = Head mass (320-380g)
d = Distance from CG to hosel pivot point

Topspin Analysis:

ω = v / R × COR

Where:
ω = Angular velocity (topspin)
v = Ball linear velocity
R = Ball radius
COR = Coefficient of restitution (target: 0.85)

Target specifications achieved initial topspin ≥15 rad/s for immediate forward roll on green surfaces.

Key Achievements:

  • Successfully designed putter head with MOI ≥5000 g·cm²
  • Achieved adjustable toe hang through weight port system
  • Validated COR consistency across face impact zone
  • Low CG position ensures immediate forward roll

Lessons Learned:

  • Parametric Design Power: Fusion 360's parametric approach allowed rapid iteration, reducing design cycle time by ~40%
  • Tolerance Sensitivity: Weight port machining requires tight tolerances (±0.1mm) for predictable toe hang adjustment
  • MATLAB Integration: Numerical modeling predicted MOI within 3% of physical testing, validating simulation approach
  • Material Properties: 3D printing material anisotropy required design adjustments for impact loading directions

Conclusions and Future Work

The Zero Torque Putter design successfully addresses the key challenges of consistent distance control and directional stability. The parametric CAD approach enables continued refinement of:

Future Optimization Areas:

1.Weight Distribution Tuning: Fine-tune toe hang range to optimize for specific putting styles
2.COR Uniformity Enhancement: Explore milled face alternatives to grooved design
3.Machining Integration: Transition from 3D printed prototype to CNC-machined aluminum or stainless steel production head
4.Shaft Compatibility: Design hosel interface for aftermarket shaft/grip combinations
